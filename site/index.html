A benchmark for evidence-supported physics research

From research goals
to scientific closure.

Can AI agents do more than produce plausible calculations—can they establish a well-supported scientific answer?

PhysClosureBench leaves the investigation to the agent. Across 50 physics tasks, we evaluate both the scientific work accomplished and whether the research question is actually resolved.

01 / What we evaluate

A research goal.
Not a prescribed solution path.

The task fixes the question and essential physical constraints. The agent chooses how to derive, compute, compare, visualize, and validate.

Tasks span strong-field atomic and molecular physics, attosecond science, solid-state physics, quantum optics, and neighboring light–matter regimes. Each attempt runs independently in a network-disabled environment with task-specific CPU and wall-time limits. Failed approaches and assessable partial work remain part of the evaluation.

LOCAL SCIENTIFIC ACCOMPLISHMENT

Atomic Score 0–10

Twenty task-specific half-point criteria record the scientific work accomplished.

F FormulationO OutputI IdentificationM MechanismV Validation / scope

Applicable criteria assess material scientific requirements, not a mandatory workflow. Conditional N/A treatment follows the published grading protocol.

OVERALL RESEARCH COMPLETION

Research Closure RC0–RC4

Does the investigation satisfy the evidence and scope conditions needed to answer its research question?

  1. RC4Complete closure
  2. RC3Main conclusion supported; validation or scope gaps remain
  3. RC2Core evidence present; a central requirement remains unresolved
  4. RC1An essential primary output or branch is missing
  5. RC0Central scientific object invalid or absent

Two complementary assessments, not two versions of the same score. RC is task-specific; it is neither a score threshold nor a cap on Atomic Score.

CASE 30 · ATTOCLOCK SCATTERING

When can a scattering model account for an attoclock offset?

An electron’s outgoing direction is shifted in an attoclock measurement. Can scattering after release explain how that shift changes with laser intensity—and does the explanation survive when the binding interaction is screened?

05 / Use & reproduce

Start with a goal.
Keep the evidence.

01

Choose a task

Give the agent the task specification, resource limits, and common submission requirements—not the rubric or source references.

02

Run independently

Use an isolated, network-disabled workspace. Preserve the agent’s report, code, figures, data, and relevant execution records.

03

Assess the evidence

Apply the task-specific rubric with an independent first pass and targeted review; adjudicate only material disagreements.

What does the human consistency check establish?

Five physics-trained experts reviewed 100 cards spanning all 50 tasks, producing 200 selected criterion-level judgments. Agreement was 81.8% overall and 75.9% for criteria explicitly linked to closure. This checks selected local grading decisions, including some supporting RC; it is not independent human reassignment of complete RC0–RC4 grades.
