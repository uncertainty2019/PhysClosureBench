# When can a scattering model account for an attoclock offset?

## Background

In an attoclock measurement, the maximum of the photoelectron momentum
distribution is displaced from the direction expected from the laser vector
potential at ionization. That displacement is often discussed as a timing
observable, but the outgoing electron also scatters from the residual binding
potential.

## Research problem

For a short circularly polarized pulse, determine whether the intensity
dependence of the offset angle can be accounted for by a post-release
central-potential scattering model. Establish how far a conclusion obtained
for an unscreened Coulomb target remains valid as the interaction is screened,
and state what the resulting evidence does and does not establish about a
universal interpretation of the attoclock offset.

Choose the derivation, numerical evidence, comparisons and presentation needed
to make that assessment scientifically convincing. The number and arrangement
of figures are for you to decide.

## Physical inputs

Work in atomic units. Use a circular driver of angular frequency
$\omega=0.057$ and the intensity convention

$$
I=2E_0^2,\qquad A_0=\frac{E_0}{\omega},
$$

where $E_0$ and $A_0$ are the peak electric-field and vector-potential
amplitudes. Study a one-electron target with ionization potential
$I_p=0.5$ under both

$$
V_C(r)=-\frac{1}{r}
$$

and the screened family

$$
V_{\lambda}(r)=-\frac{Z_{\lambda}}{r}e^{-r/\lambda}.
$$

For every finite $\lambda$, determine $Z_{\lambda}$ so that the field-free
ground-state binding energy remains $I_p=0.5$. Include the Coulomb limit, the
strong-screening case $\lambda=1$, and enough intermediate screening lengths
of your choice to test whether an inference from either endpoint transfers
across the family.
